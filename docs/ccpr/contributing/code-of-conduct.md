# CCPR Community Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

We as members, contributors, and maintainers of the Central Code Prompt Repository (CCPR) pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our Standards

### Positive Behavior Examples
- Using welcoming and inclusive language
- Being respectful of differing viewpoints and experiences
- Gracefully accepting constructive criticism
- Focusing on what is best for the community
- Showing empathy towards other community members
- Providing constructive and specific feedback
- Acknowledging mistakes and learning from them
- Supporting new contributors and helping them succeed

### Unacceptable Behavior Examples
- The use of sexualized language or imagery and unwelcome sexual attention or advances
- Trolling, insulting/derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission
- Dismissive or condescending language ("you should know this", "that's obvious")
- Repeatedly interrupting or talking over others
- Unwelcome comments regarding a person's lifestyle choices and practices
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Community-Specific Guidelines

### Prompt Development Standards
- **Quality First**: All contributions should meet high standards for clarity, accuracy, and usefulness
- **Inclusive Design**: Create prompts that work for diverse users and use cases
- **Collaborative Spirit**: Work together to improve prompts rather than competing
- **Knowledge Sharing**: Help others learn prompt engineering best practices

### Review and Feedback Culture
- **Constructive Criticism**: Focus on the work, not the person
- **Specific Suggestions**: Provide actionable feedback with concrete examples
- **Positive Recognition**: Acknowledge good work and improvements
- **Patient Teaching**: Help newcomers understand standards and processes

### Communication Guidelines
```markdown
✅ Good Communication:
"This prompt could be more effective with specific output format requirements. 
For example, you might specify JSON structure like this: {...}"

"Great work on the compliance documentation! This will really help users 
understand the requirements."

"I noticed this example might not cover edge cases. Could we add an example 
with ambiguous input?"

❌ Poor Communication:
"This is wrong."
"Obviously you don't understand prompting."
"This will never work."
"Why didn't you just use the existing prompt?"
```

## Enforcement Responsibilities

### Community Leaders
Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

### Reporting Mechanisms
- **Minor Issues**: Address directly with the individual involved
- **Moderate Issues**: Contact your manager or team lead
- **Serious Issues**: Report to [ccpr-conduct@company.com](mailto:ccpr-conduct@company.com)
- **Urgent Safety Issues**: Contact HR or Security immediately

### What to Include in Reports
- Your contact information
- Names (real, nicknames, or pseudonyms) of any other individuals involved
- Your account of what occurred, including any available records (e.g., screenshots, logs)
- Any additional information that may be helpful

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### 1. Correction
**Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning
**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary Ban
**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent Ban
**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

## Professional Context

### Workplace Considerations
Since CCPR is an internal organizational resource:
- All interactions should maintain professional standards
- Respect organizational hierarchy while encouraging open collaboration
- Follow company policies on communication and conduct
- Consider the impact of your behavior on team dynamics and productivity

### Cross-Department Collaboration
- Respect different departments' perspectives and constraints
- Use clear, jargon-free communication when working across teams
- Be patient with varying levels of technical expertise
- Acknowledge different priorities and timelines

## Privacy and Confidentiality

### Information Sharing
- Respect confidential information shared in CCPR communications
- Do not share proprietary prompts or methods outside the organization
- Obtain permission before sharing others' contributions externally
- Follow company data protection and security policies

### Attribution and Recognition
- Give proper credit for others' contributions
- Don't claim ownership of collaborative work
- Respect intellectual property guidelines
- Acknowledge inspiration and sources appropriately

## Positive Community Building

### Welcoming New Contributors
- Offer help and guidance to newcomers
- Share resources and learning materials
- Provide patient, detailed explanations
- Celebrate first contributions and milestones

### Knowledge Sharing Culture
- Document your learning and discoveries
- Share best practices and lessons learned
- Offer to mentor others in your areas of expertise
- Participate in community discussions and events

### Continuous Improvement
- Suggest improvements to processes and guidelines
- Participate in retrospectives and feedback sessions
- Help identify and solve community challenges
- Support initiatives that benefit the whole community

## Special Situations

### Handling Disagreements
When disagreements arise:
1. **Assume Good Intent**: Believe others are trying to help
2. **Focus on Issues**: Discuss ideas and approaches, not personalities
3. **Seek Understanding**: Ask questions to clarify different viewpoints
4. **Find Common Ground**: Look for shared goals and values
5. **Escalate Professionally**: Involve leadership when resolution isn't possible

### Managing Stress and Burnout
- Respect others' time and availability
- Don't expect immediate responses to non-urgent requests
- Offer support to team members who seem overwhelmed
- Advocate for sustainable contribution practices

### Cultural Sensitivity
- Be aware of different cultural communication styles
- Avoid assumptions based on names, locations, or backgrounds
- Use inclusive language and examples
- Respect different holidays, time zones, and working patterns
